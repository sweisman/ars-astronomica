Athanasius Kircher was a German Jesuit who spent most of his working life at the Collegio
Romano in Rome, where he taught mathematics and assembled one of the most famous museums
of curiosities in Europe. He published some forty large illustrated books, on magnetism,
optics, music, Egyptian hieroglyphs, China and much else, and corresponded with scholars
and missionaries across the world. In 1638 he had himself lowered into the crater of
Vesuvius to see a volcano from inside. The *Mundus Subterraneus* grew out of that descent:
an attempt to explain everything beneath the Earth's surface as one connected system.

## In the corpus

- [*Mundus Subterraneus*](/works/athanasius-kircher-mundus-subterraneus/) (1665). An anatomy of the Earth's interior, from volcanoes, underground rivers, minerals and fossils to alchemy, begun after a descent into the crater of Vesuvius.
