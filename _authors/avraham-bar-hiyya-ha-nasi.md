R. Avraham bar Ḥiyya lived in Barcelona in the early twelfth century and was known to
Latin readers as Savasorda, from an Arabic court title. He belonged to the first
generation of Jewish scholars in Christian Spain to write science in Hebrew rather than
Arabic, and much of the Hebrew scientific vocabulary that later authors used begins with
him. His works cover geometry, astronomy, the calendar and philosophy. His treatise on
practical geometry was translated into Latin by Plato of Tivoli and became a source for
Fibonacci. The three works here are his cosmology, his astronomy, and the earliest
systematic Hebrew book on the calendar.

## In the corpus

- [*Cheshbon Mahalechot HaKochavim*](/works/avraham-bar-hiyya-ha-nasi-cheshbon-mahalechot-hakochavim/) (c. 1136). Twenty gates of mathematical astronomy in Hebrew, from arithmetic and chords to the planets' courses and the conversion of Hebrew, Muslim and Roman eras. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Avraham_bar_Hiyya_ha-Nasi_Cheshbon_Mahalechot_HaKochavim.pdf)
- [*Sefer HaIbbur*](/works/avraham-bar-hiyya-ha-nasi-sefer-haibbur/) (c. 1122). The earliest systematic Hebrew treatise on the science of the calendar, and the model later calendar scholars followed for centuries. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Avraham_bar_Hiyya_ha-Nasi_Sefer_HaIbbur.pdf)
- [*Sefer Tsurat HaArets*](/works/avraham-bar-hiyya-ha-nasi-sefer-tsurat-haarets/) (c. 1132). One of the first scientific works written in Hebrew rather than Arabic: the shape of the cosmos, the paths of Sun and Moon, and the inhabited world. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Avraham_bar_Hiyya_ha-Nasi_Sefer_Tsurat_HaArets.pdf)
