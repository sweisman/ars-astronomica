R. Avraham ibn Ezra was born in Tudela and spent his first fifty years in Spain as a poet.
Around 1140 he left, and for the rest of his life he travelled, through Italy, Provence,
northern France and England, writing as he went. He is best known for his commentaries on
the Bible, which are still printed beside the text. To the Hebrew-reading communities he
passed through he also brought the mathematics, astronomy and astrology of the Arabic
world, in short books written for local patrons and often revised from one city to the
next. *Keli HaNechoshet*, on the astrolabe, is one of them.

## In the corpus

- [*Keli HaNechoshet*](/works/avraham-ibn-ezra-keli-hanechoshet/) (c. 1148). The earliest surviving Hebrew treatise on the astrolabe: how to build the instrument and use it, with a star catalogue. A matching astrolabe surfaced in 2024. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Avraham_ibn_Ezra_Keli_HaNechoshet.pdf)
