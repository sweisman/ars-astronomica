Christoph Clavius was born in Bamberg, entered the Jesuit order in 1555, and taught
mathematics at the Collegio Romano in Rome for most of his life. His textbooks, on Euclid,
arithmetic, algebra, the sphere and the astrolabe, were used in Jesuit colleges across
Europe and carried by missionaries as far as China, and they made mathematics a serious
part of the order's curriculum. He was the principal mathematician of the commission that
reformed the calendar in 1582, and spent years afterwards explaining and defending it. In
old age he confirmed Galileo's telescopic discoveries with his own instruments. The *Opera
Mathematica*, printed at Mainz in 1611 and 1612, collects his work in five volumes.

## In the corpus

- [*Opera Mathematica, Tomus Primus (Euclid, Theodosius, trigonometry)*](/works/christoph-clavius-opera-mathematica-tomus-primus/) (1611). The standard Renaissance Euclid, a commentary on Theodosius's Spherics, and the trigonometry of sines, tangents and secants. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Christoph_Clavius_Opera_Mathematica_Tomus_Primus.pdf)
- [*Opera Mathematica, Tomus Secundus (practical geometry, arithmetic, algebra)*](/works/christoph-clavius-opera-mathematica-tomus-secundus/) (1611). The applied mathematics of the Jesuit colleges: surveying and measurement, a full course in computation, and cossist algebra. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Christoph_Clavius_Opera_Mathematica_Tomus_Secundus.pdf)
- [*Opera Mathematica, Tomus Tertius (Sacrobosco's Sphere, astrolabe)*](/works/christoph-clavius-opera-mathematica-tomus-tertius/) (1611). The most influential Renaissance astronomy textbook, a commentary on Sacrobosco's Sphere, with a full treatise on the astrolabe. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Christoph_Clavius_Opera_Mathematica_Tomus_Tertius.pdf)
- [*Opera Mathematica, Tomus Quartus (Gnomonice)*](/works/christoph-clavius-opera-mathematica-tomus-quartus/) (1612). Eight books on sundials, the most exhaustive Renaissance treatment of their geometry, with an instrument for laying them out. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Christoph_Clavius_Opera_Mathematica_Tomus_Quartus.pdf)
- [*Opera Mathematica, Tomus Quintus (Gregorian calendar reform)*](/works/christoph-clavius-opera-mathematica-tomus-quintus/) (1612). The architect of the Gregorian calendar explains it in full, then defends it against Maestlin, Scaliger and Viète. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Christoph_Clavius_Opera_Mathematica_Tomus_Quintus.pdf)
