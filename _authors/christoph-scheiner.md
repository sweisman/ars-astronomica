Christoph Scheiner was a Swabian Jesuit who taught mathematics at Ingolstadt, where in
1611 he began observing spots on the Sun. His first letters on them, published under a
pseudonym, started a dispute with Galileo over who had seen the spots first and what they
were, and it soured relations between Galileo and the Jesuits for decades. Scheiner went
on observing, at Ingolstadt and later in Rome, and built instruments for the purpose. He
also invented the pantograph. The *Rosa Ursina*, printed at Bracciano between 1626 and
1630, is the result of those years.

## In the corpus

- [*Refractiones Coelestes*](/works/christoph-scheiner-refractiones-coelestes/) (1617). Why the setting Sun looks flattened: a geometrical account of refraction near the horizon, and the corrections it forces on solar measurements. - [*Rosa Ursina sive Sol*](/works/christoph-scheiner-rosa-ursina-sive-sol/) (1630). The first comprehensive treatise on sunspots: proof that the Sun rotates, the tilt of its axis, and a quarrel with Galileo over who saw the spots first.
