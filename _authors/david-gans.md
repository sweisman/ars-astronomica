David Gans was born in Lippstadt in Westphalia and studied in Kraków under R. Moses
Isserles before settling in Prague, where he was a student of the
[Maharal](/authors/judah-loew-ben-bezalel/). He wrote on history, mathematics and
astronomy at a time when few Ashkenazi scholars did. In Bohemia he came to know [Tycho
Brahe](/authors/tycho-brahe/) and Johannes Kepler and visited their observatory at
Benátky, and he describes what he saw there. *Tzemach David*, printed in 1592, is a
chronicle in two parts, Jewish history and world history. *Nechmad VeNa'im*, his astronomy, was finished near
the end of his life and not printed until 1743.

## In the corpus

- [*Nechmad VeNa'im*](/works/david-gans-nechmad-venaim/) (c. 1613). A Hebrew textbook of astronomy by a man who visited Tycho Brahe's observatory, with the author's own preface restored from a single surviving prospectus. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/David_Gans_Nechmad_VeNaim.pdf)
- [*Tzemach David*](/works/david-gans-tzemach-david/) (1592). The first history book printed in Hebrew: Jewish history in one part, world history in the other, and a preface defending the study of the gentile past. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/David_Gans_Tzemach_David.pdf)
