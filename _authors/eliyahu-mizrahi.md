R. Eliyahu Mizrahi was born in Constantinople and became the leading rabbinic authority of
the Ottoman Empire, serving as its chief rabbi until his death. Among students of the
Torah he is known for his supercommentary on Rashi, still a standard work. He was also one
of the foremost Jewish mathematicians of his age: he studied under [Mordecai
Comtino](/authors/mordecai-comtino/), wrote a commentary on Ptolemy's *Almagest*, and
composed a full treatise on arithmetic, the *Sefer HaMispar*. The *Kitsur HaMelakhat
HaMispar* presents that arithmetic in compendium form.

## In the corpus

- [*Kitsur HaMelakhat HaMispar*](/works/eliyahu-mizrahi-kitsur-hamelakhat-hamispar/) (c. 1500). Arithmetic taught step by step by the chief rabbi of the Ottoman Empire: the operations, fractions, proportion and roots, with methods for checking each.
