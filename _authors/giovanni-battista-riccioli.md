Giovanni Battista Riccioli was born in Ferrara, entered the Jesuit order at sixteen, and
worked for most of his life in Bologna. With his fellow Jesuit Francesco Maria Grimaldi he
carried out long programmes of observation and experiment, among them careful measurements
of falling bodies and of the pendulum. The *Almagestum Novum* of 1651 set out the whole of
astronomy as it then stood, with 126 arguments for and against the motion of the Earth; he
concluded for a modified form of [Tycho Brahe](/authors/tycho-brahe/)'s system. Its map of
the Moon introduced the names astronomers still use. The *Astronomia Reformata* followed
in 1665.

## In the corpus

- [*Almagestum Novum, Tomus Primus, Pars Posterior*](/works/giovanni-battista-riccioli-almagestum-novum-tomus-primus-pars-posterior/) (1651). Comets, new stars and the systems of the world, with 126 arguments for and against the Earth's motion, set out fairly enough that both sides read them. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Giovanni_Battista_Riccioli_Almagestum_Novum_Tomus_Primus_Pars_Posterior.pdf)
- [*Almagestum Novum, Tomus Primus, Pars Prior*](/works/giovanni-battista-riccioli-almagestum-novum-tomus-primus-pars-prior/) (1651). The Jesuit encyclopedia of astronomy: the sphere, Sun, Moon, eclipses, stars and planets, with the map whose crater names astronomers still use. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Giovanni_Battista_Riccioli_Almagestum_Novum_Tomus_Primus_Pars_Prior.pdf)
- [*Astronomia Reformata*](/works/giovanni-battista-riccioli-astronomia-reformata/) (1665). Telescopic observations of Saturn, Jupiter and the fixed stars, reduced to tables and marshalled against a moving Earth. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Giovanni_Battista_Riccioli_Astronomia_Reformata.pdf)
