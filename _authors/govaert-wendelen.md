Govaert Wendelen was a Flemish priest and astronomer, born at Herk-de-Stad, who observed
for more than half a century from the parishes he served. He was one of the most careful
observers of his generation and a correspondent of
[Riccioli](/authors/giovanni-battista-riccioli/), [Gassendi](/authors/pierre-gassendi/)
and Mersenne. He accepted the motion of the Earth, repeated Aristarchus's method for the
distance of the Sun and arrived at a figure far larger than any before it, and showed that
Kepler's third law holds for the moons of Jupiter. The astronomical tables he planned, the
*Tabulae Atlanticae*, were the goal; the three tracts here were published along the way.

## In the corpus

- [*Arcanorum Caelestium Lampas*](/works/govaert-wendelen-arcanorum-caelestium-lampas/) (1643). Four astronomical claims hidden in Latin hexameters, then revealed by anagram, to stake the author's priority before his tables appeared. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Govaert_Wendelen_Arcanorum_Caelestium_Lampas.pdf)
- [*Eclipses Lunares*](/works/govaert-wendelen-eclipses-lunares/) (1644). Seventy years of lunar-eclipse observations, 1573 to 1643, laid down as the foundation for a new set of astronomical tables. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Govaert_Wendelen_Eclipses_Lunares.pdf)
- [*Loxias*](/works/govaert-wendelen-loxias/) (1626). Every measurement of the tilt of the ecliptic from Eratosthenes to Tycho, reviewed, and a rule for how that angle changes over time. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Govaert_Wendelen_Loxias.pdf)
