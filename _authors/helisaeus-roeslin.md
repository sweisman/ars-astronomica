Helisaeus Roeslin was a physician and astrologer, born in Württemberg and trained at
Tübingen, who spent most of his working life in Alsace, at Haguenau and later at
Bouxwiller. He wrote on the new star of 1572 and the comet of 1577. In *De opere Dei
creationis* (1597) he set the rival systems of the world side by side and added one of
his own, in which the Sun and Moon circle a resting Earth and the other planets circle
the Sun. It stands close to the systems of [Tycho Brahe](/authors/tycho-brahe/) and of
[Ursus](/authors/nicolaus-reimers-ursus/), and the book was drawn into their quarrel.
In later years he and Kepler argued in print over astrology and the new star of 1604.

## In the corpus

- [*De Opere Dei Creationis*](/works/helisaeus-roeslin-de-opere-dei-creationis/) (1597). Five systems of the world set side by side in 1597: Ptolemy, Copernicus, Ursus, Tycho and the author's own, in which the planets circle a moving Sun.
