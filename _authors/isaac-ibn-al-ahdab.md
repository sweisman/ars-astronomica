R. Isaac ben Solomon ibn al-Aḥdab was born in Castile in the middle of the fourteenth
century and left it for Sicily, where he was living by the 1390s. He was a poet as well as
a scientist. His *Iggeret HaMispar*, a Hebrew reworking of an Arabic arithmetic by Ibn
al-Bannāʾ, includes one of the earliest treatments of algebra in Hebrew. The two works
here are astronomical: the *Orah Selulah*, tables for the conjunctions and oppositions of
Sun and Moon that survive in some twenty-five manuscripts, and the *Keli Ḥemda*, on an
instrument for finding the planets' positions without tables.

## In the corpus

- [*Keli Ḥemda*](/works/isaac-ibn-al-ahdab-keli-hemda/) (late 14th c.). How to make and use the equatorium, an instrument that finds planetary positions geometrically rather than by table. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Isaac_ibn_al-Ahdab_Keli_Hemda.pdf)
- [*Orah Selulah*](/works/isaac-ibn-al-ahdab-orah-selulah/) (c. 1396). Tables for the true conjunctions and oppositions of Sun and Moon, among the most widely copied Hebrew table-works, in some twenty-five manuscripts. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Isaac_ibn_al-Ahdab_Orah_Selulah.pdf)
