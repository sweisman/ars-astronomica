R. Jacob ben Machir belonged to the Ibn Tibbon family of translators and lived at
Montpellier, where Latin scholars knew him as Profatius Judaeus. He translated Euclid, and
Arabic works on astronomy and the sphere, into Hebrew. Two works of his own travelled far
beyond the Hebrew-reading world. One is the *Roba' Yisrael*, the quadrant of Israel: an
instrument of his invention that folds the working parts of an astrolabe onto a
quarter-circle, and which passed into Latin as the *quadrans novus*. The other is his
Almanac of 1300, a set of planetary tables that Latin astronomers used for generations.

## In the corpus

- [*Roba' Yisrael*](/works/jacob-ben-machir-ibn-tibbon-roba-yisrael/) (c. 1288). An improved astronomical quadrant of the author's own invention, in sixteen gates. It passed into Latin and was cited by Renaissance astronomers. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Jacob_ben_Machir_ibn_Tibbon_Roba_Yisrael.pdf)
