Johannes Hevelius was a brewer and city councillor of Danzig who built, across the roofs
of three adjoining houses, the largest observatory in Europe after [Tycho
Brahe](/authors/tycho-brahe/)'s Uraniborg. He ground his own lenses, built his own
instruments, and engraved his own plates. The *Selenographia* of 1647, his first major
book, mapped the Moon through its phases; the *Cometographia* of 1668 surveyed the comets.
He went on measuring star positions with open sights after others had moved to telescopic
ones, a choice that Robert Hooke attacked and that Edmond Halley came to Danzig to test.
In 1679 a fire destroyed the observatory; he rebuilt it and worked until his death.

## In the corpus

- [*Cometographia*](/works/johannes-hevelius-cometographia/) (1668). The major survey of comets before Newton, arguing from a rooftop observatory that they travel curved, near-parabolic paths. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Johannes_Hevelius_Cometographia.pdf)
- [*Machina Coelestis, Pars Prior*](/works/johannes-hevelius-machina-coelestis-pars-prior/) (1673). The instruments of Europe's largest observatory after Uraniborg, whose defence of naked-eye sights drew Hooke's attack and Halley's visit. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Johannes_Hevelius_Machina_Coelestis_Pars_Prior.pdf)
- [*Selenographia*](/works/johannes-hevelius-selenographia/) (1647). The first lunar atlas drawn from systematic telescopic observation, the Moon night by night through its phases, engraved by the astronomer himself. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Johannes_Hevelius_Selenographia.pdf)
