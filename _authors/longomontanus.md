Christen Sørensen, called Longomontanus after his native village of Lomborg in Jutland,
was a farmer's son who became [Tycho Brahe](/authors/tycho-brahe/)'s principal assistant.
He worked on Hven from 1589 until Tycho left Denmark, rejoined him in Bohemia, and carried
much of the computation behind the lunar theory and the star catalogue. After Tycho's
death the observations passed to Kepler; Longomontanus returned to Denmark, became
professor of mathematics at Copenhagen, and wrote the book his master had not lived to
write. The *Astronomia Danica* of 1622 sets out the whole of astronomy on Tychonic
foundations. He later directed the building of the Round Tower observatory in Copenhagen.

## In the corpus

- [*Astronomia Danica*](/works/longomontanus-astronomia-danica/) (1622). The fullest account of the Tychonic system, by the assistant who computed at Tycho's side, with tables, trigonometry, new stars and comets. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Longomontanus_Astronomia_Danica.pdf)
