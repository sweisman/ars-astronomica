Nicolaus Reimers, called Bär and in Latin Ursus, the bear, came from Dithmarschen and
by his own account herded pigs until he was eighteen. He taught himself Latin, Greek and
mathematics, worked as a surveyor, and in 1584 visited [Tycho
Brahe](/authors/tycho-brahe/)'s observatory on Hven. Four years later his *Fundamentum
Astronomicum* published a system of the world very like Tycho's, and Tycho accused him of
having stolen it during that visit. The quarrel lasted the rest of both their lives. Ursus
became imperial mathematician to Rudolf II in 1591; when Tycho came to Prague, Kepler was
set to write the case against him.

## In the corpus

- [*Fundamentum Astronomicum*](/works/nicolaus-reimers-ursus-fundamentum-astronomicum/) (1588). A new method of sines and triangles, and the world system Tycho Brahe accused its author of stealing. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Nicolaus_Reimers_Ursus_Fundamentum_Astronomicum.pdf)
