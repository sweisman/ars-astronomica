Pedro Nunes was born at Alcácer do Sal, studied at Salamanca and Lisbon, and became royal
cosmographer of Portugal and professor of mathematics at Coimbra. His subject was the
mathematics the Portuguese voyages needed. He showed that a ship holding a constant
compass bearing does not sail a great circle but a spiral toward the pole, the curve later
called the loxodrome. In his book on twilight he solved the problem of the shortest
twilight and described a device for reading fine divisions of a scale, the nonius, which
bears his Latin name. He gathered his Latin works at Coimbra in 1573 and saw the edition
through the press himself.
