Pierre Gassendi was a priest from Provence, provost of the cathedral of Digne, and from
1645 Royal Professor of Mathematics in Paris. As a philosopher he is known for reviving
the atomism of Epicurus in a form a Christian could hold, and for his objections to
Descartes's *Meditations*. As an astronomer he observed constantly: in 1631 he became the
first person to see a planet cross the face of the Sun, the transit of Mercury that Kepler
had predicted. The *Institutio Astronomica* is the course he dictated to his Paris
students. His life of [Tycho Brahe](/authors/tycho-brahe/), printed in 1654 with shorter
lives of Copernicus, Peurbach and Regiomontanus, is an early landmark of scientific
biography.

## In the corpus

- [*Institutio Astronomica*](/works/pierre-gassendi-institutio-astronomica/) (1647). The standard astronomy textbook of its generation, setting the Ptolemaic, Copernican and Tychonic systems side by side. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Pierre_Gassendi_Institutio_Astronomica.pdf)
- [*Tychonis Brahei Vita*](/works/pierre-gassendi-tychonis-brahei-vita/) (1654). Tycho Brahe's life, from Hven and its instruments to the final Prague years, with shorter lives of Copernicus, Peurbach and Regiomontanus. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Pierre_Gassendi_Tychonis_Brahei_Vita.pdf)
