Tycho Brahe was a Danish nobleman who gave his life to measuring the heavens more
accurately than anyone had before. The new star of 1572 and the comet of 1577, both of
which he showed to lie beyond the Moon, convinced him that the heavens change and that the
solid spheres of the old astronomy did not exist. With royal support he built Uraniborg
and Stjerneborg on the island of Hven, and for twenty-one years he and his assistants,
among them [Longomontanus](/authors/longomontanus/), observed there with instruments of
his own design. He proposed a system in which the planets circle the Sun and the Sun
circles a resting Earth. He left Denmark in 1597, became imperial mathematician to Rudolf
II, and died in Prague in 1601, leaving his observations to Johannes Kepler. [Pierre
Gassendi](/authors/pierre-gassendi/) wrote his life.

## In the corpus

- [*Astronomiae Instauratae Mechanica*](/works/tycho-brahe-astronomiae-instauratae-mechanica/) (1598). The most accurate instruments built before the telescope, each engraved and described, with a tour of Uraniborg and Stjerneborg. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Tycho_Brahe_Astronomiae_Instauratae_Mechanica.pdf)
- [*Astronomiae Instauratae Progymnasmata*](/works/tycho-brahe-astronomiae-instauratae-progymnasmata/) (1602). Solar and lunar theory, the best star catalogue before the telescope, and the new star of 1572, left unfinished and carried to press by Kepler. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Tycho_Brahe_Astronomiae_Instauratae_Progymnasmata.pdf)
- [*De Mundi Aetherei Recentioribus Phaenomenis*](/works/tycho-brahe-de-mundi-aetherei-recentioribus-phaenomenis/) (1588). The comet of 1577 showed no parallax, so it moved where the crystalline spheres were supposed to be. A new system of the world follows. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Tycho_Brahe_De_Mundi_Aetherei_Recentioribus_Phaenomenis.pdf)
- [*Epistolarum Astronomicarum Libri*](/works/tycho-brahe-epistolarum-astronomicarum-libri/) (1596). Before journals, astronomers argued by letter: two observatories, Hven and Kassel, testing each other's work in real time. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Tycho_Brahe_Epistolarum_Astronomicarum_Libri.pdf)
- [*Thesaurus Observationum, Tomus Primus*](/works/tycho-brahe-thesaurus-observationum-tomus-primus/) (1923). Tycho's observation logs from 1563 to 1585, from his first recorded observation at sixteen: the raw record behind the best naked-eye astronomy ever made. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Tycho_Brahe_Thesaurus_Observationum_Tomus_Primus.pdf)
- [*Thesaurus Observationum, Tomus Secundus*](/works/tycho-brahe-thesaurus-observationum-tomus-secundus/) (1924). Tycho's observation logs for 1586 to 1589, at the height of the Hven observatory, with a catalogue of the zodiacal stars. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Tycho_Brahe_Thesaurus_Observationum_Tomus_Secundus.pdf)
- [*Thesaurus Observationum, Tomus Tertius*](/works/tycho-brahe-thesaurus-observationum-tomus-tertius/) (1925). Tycho's observation logs for 1590 to 1595, the last full years of the Hven observatory. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Tycho_Brahe_Thesaurus_Observationum_Tomus_Tertius.pdf)
- [*Thesaurus Observationum, Tomus Quartus*](/works/tycho-brahe-thesaurus-observationum-tomus-quartus/) (1926). Tycho's observation logs for 1596 to 1601, from the last years on Hven through his exile in Germany and Bohemia. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Tycho_Brahe_Thesaurus_Observationum_Tomus_Quartus.pdf)
- [*Triangulorum Planorum et Sphaericorum Praxis Arithmetica*](/works/tycho-brahe-triangulorum-planorum-et-sphaericorum-praxis-arithmetica/) (1591). Sixteen rules for solving plane and spherical triangles by arithmetic: the working trigonometry behind the Uraniborg observations. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Tycho_Brahe_Triangulorum_Planorum_et_Sphaericorum_Praxis_Arithmetica.pdf)
