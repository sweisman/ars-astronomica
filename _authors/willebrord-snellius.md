Willebrord Snellius succeeded his father as professor of mathematics at Leiden. In 1615 he
measured an arc of the meridian between Alkmaar and Bergen op Zoom by chaining triangles
across the towns of Holland, the method on which geodesy rested for the next three
centuries; *Eratosthenes Batavus* is his account of it. Around 1621 he found the law
governing the refraction of light that now carries his name, though he never published it.
He was also an editor: he saw into print the observations made at Kassel under Landgrave
Wilhelm IV, which with [Tycho Brahe](/authors/tycho-brahe/)'s were the best made before
the telescope.

## In the corpus

- [*Eratosthenes Batavus*](/works/willebrord-snellius-eratosthenes-batavus/) (1617). How the Earth was measured by chaining triangles across Holland, the survey that founded modern geodesy. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Willebrord_Snellius_Eratosthenes_Batavus.pdf)
- [*Observationes Hassiacae*](/works/willebrord-snellius-observationes-hassiacae/) (1618). The Kassel observations of Landgrave Wilhelm IV, the other great body of pre-telescopic precision, with Regiomontanus's Nuremberg observations. [PDF](https://books.arsastronomica.com/Willebrord_Snellius_Observationes_Hassiacae.pdf)
