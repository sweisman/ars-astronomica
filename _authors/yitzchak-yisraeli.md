R. Yitzchak Yisraeli, called the Younger to distinguish him from the tenth-century
physician of the same name, lived in Toledo in the early fourteenth century. He was a
student of R. Asher ben Yechiel, the Rosh, who had come to Toledo from Germany, and it was
at the Rosh's request that he wrote *Yesod Olam*. The book builds from geometry and
trigonometry through the motions of the Sun and Moon to the rules of the Hebrew calendar,
and closes with a chronicle of the transmission of the tradition down to his own day. It
remained a standard authority on the calendar for centuries.
