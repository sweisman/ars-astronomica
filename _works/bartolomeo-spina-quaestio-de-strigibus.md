Bartolomeo Spina (c. 1474–1546) was a Dominican theologian who would become
Master of the Sacred Palace. His *Quaestio de strigibus*, first printed at Venice
in 1523, is the Dominican answer to the sceptics: it holds that the *strigae* are
real, that they travel bodily to their gatherings, copulate with demons and
murder infants, and that they are therefore heretics and apostates whom the
secular arm must burn.

## What is in this book

The *Quaestio* argues from Aquinas and the canonists, but its most striking pages
are the depositions: cases from Ferrara, Bergamo and the Val Camonica that Spina
had heard of at first or second hand and sets down as evidence. The volume also
carries his *Tractatus de praeeminentia sacrae theologiae*, in two tractates, and
four *Apologiae* against the jurist Gianfrancesco Ponzinibio, who had argued that
the witches' flight was a dream and that the Inquisition had no jurisdiction over
it.

## Why it matters

Spina's reply to Ponzinibio is one of the fiercest exchanges of the Italian
witch debate. It turns on the question that divided the learned for a century:
whether what the accused confessed had happened in the body or only in the mind,
and so whether it was a crime at all.

## About this edition

The source is the Rome collected edition of 1576, from the Bavarian State
Library, translated in full: the *Quaestio*, the *De praeeminentia* and the four
*Apologiae*. No digitization of the 1523 Venice first edition was located.
