The *Almagestum Novum* (Bologna, 1651) is the great Jesuit encyclopedia of
seventeenth-century astronomy, by Giovanni Battista Riccioli (1598–1671). This
second part turns from the individual heavens to the shape of the whole.

## What is in this book

Books VIII to X treat comets and new stars, the systems of the world, and a
book of practical astronomical problems.

## Why it matters

Its centre is Book IX, where Riccioli lays out 126 arguments for and against
the Earth's motion. It is the fullest case ever made against Copernicus, set out
fairly enough that both sides read it.

## About this edition

The source is the Bologna printing of 1651 in the ETH-Bibliothek Zürich. The
[first part](/works/giovanni-battista-riccioli-almagestum-novum-tomus-primus-pars-prior/),
with the Sun, the Moon, the stars, the planets and the famous map of the Moon,
is a separate volume. Riccioli's later
[*Astronomia Reformata*](/works/giovanni-battista-riccioli-astronomia-reformata/)
is also in this corpus.
