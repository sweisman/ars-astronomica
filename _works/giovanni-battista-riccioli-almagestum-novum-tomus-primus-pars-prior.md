The *Almagestum Novum* (Bologna, 1651) is the great Jesuit encyclopedia of
seventeenth-century astronomy. Its author, Giovanni Battista Riccioli
(1598–1671), took its name from Ptolemy's ancient masterpiece and aimed to do for
his own age what the *Almagest* did for antiquity: to set out the whole of
astronomy as it then stood.

## What is in this book

This first part, Books I to VII, covers the sphere and the Earth, the Sun, the
Moon, eclipses, the fixed stars and the five planets. It opens with Riccioli's
preface, his indexes, and a double chronicle of the astronomers who came before
him.

## Why it matters

This part contains the map of the Moon that Riccioli drew with his colleague
Francesco Maria Grimaldi. Its names for the craters and seas, among them Tycho,
Copernicus, Plato and the Sea of Tranquility, are the names astronomers still
use.

## About this edition

The source is the Bologna printing of 1651. The
[second part](/works/giovanni-battista-riccioli-almagestum-novum-tomus-primus-pars-posterior/)
of the first volume, with the comets, the systems of the world and the 126
arguments on the Earth's motion, is a separate volume. Riccioli's later
[*Astronomia Reformata*](/works/giovanni-battista-riccioli-astronomia-reformata/)
is also in this corpus.
