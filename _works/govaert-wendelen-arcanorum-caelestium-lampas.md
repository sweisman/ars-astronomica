Govaert Wendelen (Godefridus Wendelinus, 1580–1667) was a Flemish priest and
astronomer. In this short tract, the "four-wicked lamp" of heavenly secrets, he
publishes four astronomical claims in the manner of his age: first hidden in
four Latin hexameters, the "shadows," then revealed by anagram, the "lights."

## What is in this book

Each pair of lines is made of the same letters: 36, 37, 32 and 37. Revealed,
the four lights claim that all natural days are equal; that an equal oscillation
drives the Moon at its highest and its lowest; that a sextantal ratio governs the
apsides; and that the Moon triples the year's turns at the apsis of the Sun.
Each light is followed by a brief explanation. The tract is dedicated to the
humanist Erycius Puteanus and carries a letter from Puteanus to the reader.

## Why it matters

The *Lampas* records the priority claims Wendelen staked out while preparing his
*Tabulae Atlanticae*, and it is a curiosity of seventeenth-century scientific
publishing.
