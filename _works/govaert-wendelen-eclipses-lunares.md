Govaert Wendelen (Godefridus Wendelinus, 1580–1667) was a Flemish priest and
astronomer, one of the most careful observers of his generation and a
correspondent of Riccioli, Gassendi and Mersenne. *Eclipses lunares* collects
observations of lunar eclipses made between 1573 and 1643 and presents them as
the observational foundation for his projected astronomical tables, the
*Tabulae Atlanticae*.

## What is in this book

The book opens with the judgements of fellow mathematicians on Wendelen's work,
among them Petavius and Malapert, and with his preface. Then come his methods:
the places of observation and the differences of their meridians, how to observe
the time, and the plan of the Atlantic and lunar tables. The body of the book is
the eclipses themselves, Tycho's and those observed chiefly throughout Europe in
Wendelen's own lifetime, each set out in turn, with a few solar eclipses
among them.

## Why it matters

The book belongs to the mid-seventeenth-century effort to rebuild astronomical
tables on fresh, well-dated observations.
