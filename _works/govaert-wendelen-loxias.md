Govaert Wendelen (Godefridus Wendelinus, 1580–1667) was a Flemish priest and
astronomer. *Loxias*, "the oblique one," an epithet of Apollo, is his treatise
on the obliquity of the ecliptic: the angle between the Sun's annual path and
the celestial equator.

## What is in this book

Wendelen opens with the observations of his predecessors, from Eratosthenes and
Ptolemy through al-Battānī, al-Zarqālī, Peuerbach, Copernicus and Tycho, to his
own measurements at Forcalquier in Provence between 1609 and 1612. From Eratosthenes
to Peuerbach he finds a steady decrease; after Peuerbach the figures rise and
fall. He concludes that the obliquity is not fixed, and in seven chapters works
out a rule for its variation. The book closes with a table of the solar
obliquities and instructions for its use.

## Why it matters

*Loxias* is an early landmark in the long debate over whether, and how, the
obliquity changes.
