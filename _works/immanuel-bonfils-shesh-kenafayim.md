One of the most widely copied Hebrew astronomical handbooks of the late Middle
Ages, composed around 1365 in Provence. Its six sets of tables — the "wings" that
give the work its name — let the user compute the positions of the sun and moon,
predict eclipses, and convert between calendars, each with brief instructions for
use.

## What is in this book

The book is almost entirely apparatus: a short canon explaining the method,
then wing after wing of tables. The First Wing is a cycle-table, with an annual
table on a twenty-eight-year cycle and monthly tables for common and leap years.
The Second Wing carries the lunar *ḥeq* — the conjunction tables — across many
folios, in degrees, with separate variant groups for leap years. Later wings give
the corrected lunar reckoning, laid out in sign-panels running through the zodiac,
Virgo to Libra and onward.

The manuscript's physical character is part of the work: rubricated header bands
in red and gold, diagonal panel sub-headers repeated across each panel,
header-letter rows in gold and red. These tables were made to be consulted
quickly, and the colour is navigational rather than decorative.

## Why it matters

*Shesh Kenafayim* was a working tool, and its diffusion shows it: it survives in
many copies, and was translated into both Greek and Latin — an unusual direction
of travel for a Hebrew scientific text. Its most striking technical feature is the use
of a decimal notation for astronomical fractions, long before such methods spread
through European mathematics. Bonfils was reaching for something that would
not become general practice for centuries.

## About this edition

This English version is drawn from an Italian manuscript copy made in 1509 —
some hundred and fifty years after composition — held at the University of
Pennsylvania's Kislak Center. The work also circulated under the name *Kanfei
Nesharim*, "Wings of Eagles."
