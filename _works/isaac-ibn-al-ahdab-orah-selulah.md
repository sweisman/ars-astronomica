A set of astronomical tables by Isaac ibn al-Aḥdab (Sicily, late 14th c.) for
computing the true conjunctions and oppositions of the Sun and Moon — the
*Orah Selulah*, "The Paved Way," one of the most widely copied Hebrew
astronomical table-works, surviving in some twenty-five manuscripts.

## What is in this text

A short preface and a pair of opening gates explain the method; everything after
that is table. The body is built from lunar and solar equation tables — the
argument-correction tables (*luaḥ ḥeq levanah*) and double-entry equation tables
that turn a mean position into a true one — laid out with rubricated headers in
red so a user can find the right column at speed. A planetary-hours table sits
among them.

The title is an argument in miniature. A conjunction or opposition can be
computed from first principles, but the work is long and the chances of error
many; a well-made table paves the way, so that the same result is reached by
lookup and interpolation. What the book offers is not new astronomy but a
smoothed road through it.

## Why it matters

Conjunctions and oppositions are what the Hebrew calendar turns on — the molad
governs the months, and eclipse prediction depends on the same quantities. That
practical necessity is why table-works like this one were copied far more often
than treatises: they were used. Twenty-five surviving manuscripts is a large
number for a Hebrew scientific text, and it measures how thoroughly the *Orah
Selulah* embedded itself in practice.

## About this edition

The translated witness is the colour manuscript BnF Hébreu 1086, composed in
Syracuse around 1396 and copied later. Its front matter preserves the accidents of
a working codex — guard leaves with pen-trials and jottings, a shelfmark on a
blank flyleaf, a cataloguer's leaf — before the incipit begins.
