Johannes Hevelius's *Cometographia* (Danzig, 1668) is the major survey of comets
written before Newton. It draws on his own careful observations of the comets of
1652, 1661, 1664 and 1665, made from his rooftop observatory in Danzig.

## What is in this book

Hevelius examines the place, parallax, distance, formation and dissolution of
comets, and the changing shapes of their heads and tails. Above all he examines
their motion. The work closes with a history of every comet recorded from
antiquity to his own day, annotated by the author, and a universal table of
comets.

## Why it matters

Hevelius argued that comets follow curved, near-parabolic paths rather than
straight lines, a step toward the orbits Newton would compute a generation
later. Its many engraved plates, cut by Hevelius himself, are among the finest
astronomical illustrations of the century.
