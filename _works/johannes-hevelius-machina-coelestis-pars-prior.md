Johannes Hevelius, a Danzig brewer and city councillor, built on the roofs of
three adjoining houses the largest observatory in Europe after Tycho Brahe's
Uraniborg. The *Machina Coelestis* is his account of it.

## What is in this book

This first part, the *Organographia* of 1673, describes and illustrates every
instrument he used: sextants, quadrants, octants and the great azimuthal
quadrant, with their construction, division and use. He printed it at his own
press, with engravings after his own drawings and those of Andreas Stech.

## Why it matters

Its defence of open, naked-eye sights over telescopic ones led directly to
Robert Hooke's *Animadversions* (1674), and so to the dispute that brought
Edmond Halley to Danzig in 1679 to compare the two methods in person. It stands
beside Tycho's
[*Mechanica*](/works/tycho-brahe-astronomiae-instauratae-mechanica/) as the
other great illustrated catalogue of pre-telescopic instruments.

## About this edition

The source is the Smithsonian Libraries copy of the 1673 Danzig printing. The
*Machina Coelestis* was issued in two parts; this volume is the *Pars Prior*.
The *Pars Posterior* (1679), with the observations themselves, is a separate
work. Only three chapters have previously appeared in English (Forshall, 1882).
