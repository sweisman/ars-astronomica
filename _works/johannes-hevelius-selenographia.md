Johannes Hevelius, a Danzig brewer and city councillor, spent four years
observing the Moon through telescopes he ground and built himself before
publishing the *Selenographia* in 1647. It was his first major book.

## What is in this book

Forty engraved plates show the Moon night by night through its phases, followed
by three full-Moon maps and an account of libration, the slow rocking that
brings parts of the far side into view. Hevelius engraved the plates himself.
The book also treats the appearance of the planets and sunspots, and closes
with a method for grinding lenses and building telescopes.

## Why it matters

The *Selenographia* is the first lunar atlas drawn from systematic telescopic
observation. Many of the names Hevelius gave lunar features, borrowed from the
geography of the Earth, are still in use, though the rival nomenclature of
Riccioli's
[*Almagestum Novum*](/works/giovanni-battista-riccioli-almagestum-novum-tomus-primus-pars-prior/)
(1651), named after astronomers, largely displaced his.
