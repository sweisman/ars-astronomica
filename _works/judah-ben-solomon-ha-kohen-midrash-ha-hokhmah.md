The first scientific encyclopedia written in Hebrew. *Midrash ha-Ḥokhmah* —
"The Exposition of Wisdom" — is the work of Judah ben Solomon ha-Kohen (Ibn
Matḳah) of Toledo, who wrote it first in Arabic and then rendered it into Hebrew
himself around 1247.

## What is in this book

The work gathers the philosophical sciences as they stood in the Arabic tradition
and sets them out for a Hebrew reader in a single ordered sequence of treatises.

Its natural-science section is the most fully preserved part of this witness, and it
proceeds through the body organ by organ — the lung, the liver, the spleen, the
kidneys and bladder, the midriff, the belly — before turning to generation. There
the treatises take up the womb, the nature of semen and which organ it issues
from, the eggs of fish, the generation of bees, the shelled animals, and the causes
of male and female, of resemblance and heredity, of milk. A further treatise treats
hair: its thickness and fineness, its softness, the cause of the beard, the causes of
its colours.

This is Aristotelian biology carried into Hebrew — the questions a thirteenth-
century natural philosopher thought a complete account of living things had to
answer.

## Why it matters

Judah ha-Kohen belongs to the moment when Hebrew was becoming a language
of science, and his self-translation from his own Arabic makes the transition
visible in a single author. Encyclopedias like this one were how a Hebrew reader
without Arabic gained access to the whole Aristotelian curriculum at once, and
they shaped what later Hebrew writers — including several elsewhere in this
corpus — could assume their readers already knew.

## About this edition

The translated witness is the Parma manuscript (Cod. Parm. 2769), a fourteenth-
century copy. Its rubrics are executed in heavy display ink, and several are
abraded to the point where the reading is uncertain; the translation marks those
places rather than silently choosing.
