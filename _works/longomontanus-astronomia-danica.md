A comprehensive textbook of astronomy and the definitive technical account of
the Tychonic system — Earth at rest, the Sun circling it, the planets circling the
Sun. It is the fullest pre-Newtonian exposition of that system, written by the man
who had computed at Tycho's side.

## What is in this book

The book begins with the mathematics before it reaches the sky. Part One opens
on the compendious analysis of plane and spherical triangles by numbers,
supplying general theorems for their solution, and then sets out the
prosthaphaeretic doctrine — the technique of turning multiplication into addition
by trigonometric identity, which is how large astronomical computations were
made tractable before logarithms. A long sequence of numbered doctrines applies
this to plane and then spherical triangles in worked numeric form.

With the computational apparatus in hand, the work proceeds systematically
through the structure of the cosmos, the motions of the Sun and Moon, eclipses,
the planets, the fixed stars, and precession, with working tables and geometric
diagrams throughout. An appendix treats the new stars of 1572 and 1604 and the
comets of 1607 and 1618 — the phenomena that had broken the old cosmology
and that the Tychonic system was built to accommodate.

## Why it matters

Longomontanus had been Tycho's assistant, and *Astronomia Danica* is the
system's technical case as its own camp would have made it: not a sketch of an
arrangement, but a full working astronomy with the tables an observer needs to
predict positions. It is the book to read to understand why the Tychonic
compromise was taken seriously for as long as it was — it did the job, on data
that could not yet distinguish it from the alternatives.

## About this edition

The translated source is the 1640 printing, which opens with a dedicatory epistle
and a set of commendatory addresses to the author as professor in the Royal
Danish Academy.
