An instructional treatise on arithmetic and practical geometry by Mordecai ben
Eliezer Comtino (Constantinople, 15th c.), one of the leading Rabbanite scholars of
Byzantine Jewry. It combines numerical method with practical geometry, and no
English translation is known.

## What is in this book

The work divides into two books. The first is arithmetic, proceeding part by part
and chapter by chapter through addition, multiplication — including squaring —
and division, each carried through worked numeric examples in the manuscript's
own hand. A dedicated section of problems (*meqom ha-she'elot*) follows, where
the operations are put to use.

The second book is the science of measures — geometry. It works through
mensuration systematically: the measurement of triangles, including finding the
altitude and the segments of the base, then the measurement of quadrilaterals
and onward. The text is accompanied by labeled construction diagrams, and the
prose is written to be followed with the figure in view.

## Why it matters

Comtino taught Karaite as well as Rabbanite students, which is unusual enough to
say something about how mathematics functioned in fifteenth-century
Constantinople: it was common ground between communities that disagreed
sharply about much else. The same crossing appears elsewhere in this corpus —
Delmedigo's *Sefer Elim* is a reply to the Karaite Zerah ben Nathan — and
suggests the sciences were where these conversations could happen.

The pairing of arithmetic with practical geometry in one volume is also
characteristic: this is mathematics for people who needed to compute and
measure, in the tradition that Mizrahi, Comtino's near-contemporary in the same
city, was writing in as well.

## About this edition

The translated witness is BnF Hébreu 1031, folios 26r–65r — the same anthology
that preserves ibn al-Aḥdab's *Keli Ḥemda* and ibn Tibbon's *Roba' Yisrael*.
