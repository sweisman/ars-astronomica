Nicolas Jacquier (d. 1472) was a Dominican inquisitor who had sat on the Arras
*vauderie* trials. His *Flagellum haereticorum fascinariorum*, written in 1458,
is the first sustained inquisitorial defence of the reality of the witches'
sabbath.

## What is in this book

For centuries the *Canon Episcopi* had held that women who believed they rode out
at night with Diana were merely dreaming. Jacquier argues against it that the
gatherings of the *fascinarii* are bodily, real and heretical, and therefore fall
under the inquisitor's jurisdiction rather than the confessor's. He builds the
case chapter by chapter from scripture, the Fathers, scholastic demonology and
canon law: how demons delude the senses and assume bodies, what offerings they
demand of their devotees, how far their power over men and the elements extends,
and why God permits it. He draws too on depositions he had heard himself.

## Why it matters

First printed in 1581, more than a century after it was written, the *Flagellum*
is one of the founding texts of the learned witch-hunt.

## About this edition

The translation is made from the composite demonological volume printed at
Frankfurt in 1581, from the Bibliothèque nationale de France, and covers
Jacquier's own preliminaries and treatise. The compiler's dedication and index
to the whole volume are left out, as they belong to the collection rather than
to Jacquier's text.
