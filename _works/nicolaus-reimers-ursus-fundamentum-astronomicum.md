Nicolaus Reimers Ursus (1551–1600) was a self-taught mathematician from
Dithmarschen who later became imperial mathematician to Rudolf II. He published
the *Fundamentum astronomicum* at Strasbourg in 1588.

## What is in this book

The book sets out a new method of sines and triangles, with tables. It closes
with a new hypothesis of the world system: a geo-heliocentric arrangement in
which the planets circle the Sun, the Sun circles the Earth, and the Earth turns
daily on its axis. Three folded plates accompany the text.

## Why it matters

Tycho Brahe held that Ursus had taken the system from his papers during a visit
to Uraniborg in 1584. The charge of plagiarism opened one of the bitterest
priority disputes of early modern astronomy, to which Kepler's *Apologia pro
Tychone* belongs.

## About this edition

The source is the 1588 Strasbourg printing, from a copy in Copenhagen. A German
translation exists; this is the first in English.
