The *Institutio Astronomica* (Paris, 1647) is the course of astronomy that Pierre
Gassendi (1592–1655) dictated to his students as Royal Professor of Mathematics
in Paris. It became the standard textbook of its generation.

## What is in this book

It teaches the doctrine of the sphere, then the theories of the Sun, the Moon
and the planets. It sets the Ptolemaic, Copernican and Tychonic world systems
side by side and weighs each against observation and Scripture. The volume
closes with Gassendi's inaugural oration on the dignity of mathematics.

## Why it matters

Reprinted many times across Europe, the *Institutio* shaped how a generation
learned the heavens. It shows how a careful astronomer taught the choice between
three world systems at a moment when the Church had condemned one of them and
the observations had not yet settled the question.

## About this edition

The source is the 1647 Paris printing, with the inaugural oration reissued
alongside it. Gassendi's
