Thomas Erastus (Thomas Lieber, 1524–1583) was professor of medicine at
Heidelberg. His *Disputatio de lamiis seu strigibus* is a dialogue on what
witches can and cannot actually do.

## What is in this book

Erastus grants the premise that witches exist, have made a pact with the devil
and deserve death, and then spends the book dismantling, on medical and physical
grounds, nearly every marvel attributed to them. Transformation into wolves,
bodily flight to the sabbath, the raising of storms, intercourse with demons in
assumed bodies: these he explains as demonic illusion, melancholic delusion, or
the ordinary action of poisons on the imagination. His speaker Furnius presses
the popular and legal case; Erastus answers as a doctor.

## Why it matters

The *Disputatio* is one of the sharpest sixteenth-century analyses of the
difference between what a demon can do and what nature permits.

## About this edition

The translation is made from the composite demonological volume printed at
Frankfurt in 1581 by Nikolaus Basse, from the Bibliothèque nationale de France.
There the disputation sits, pointedly, beside the inquisitors it contradicts. Two
of them, Nicolas Jacquier's *Flagellum haereticorum fascinariorum* and Martín de
Arles's *Tractatus de superstitionibus*, are translated separately in this
series.
