A sumptuously illustrated catalog of the most accurate astronomical instruments
built before the telescope, gathered on the island observatory of Hven.

## What is in this book

The book proceeds instrument by instrument, and each entry follows the same
pattern: a finely engraved plate, then an *explicatio fabricae et usus* — an account
of its construction and use, giving dimensions, materials, mode of operation, and
the precision it could achieve.

The sequence is a survey of everything a pre-telescopic observatory needed. There
are quadrants in profusion — the lesser gilt brass quadrant, two azimuthal brass
quadrants, a revolving azimuthal quadrant, a great steel quadrant enclosed in a
steel square, and the celebrated mural or Tychonic quadrant. There are sextants,
including the astronomical sextant for altitudes and the triangular sextant for
measuring distances between stars. There are parallactic instruments, or rules,
one of them serving for both altitudes and azimuths. There is a great azimuthal
semicircle, a bipartite arc for the smaller stellar distances, and a series of
armillaries — zodiacal, equatorial, further equatorial, and the greatest equatorial
armillae of one and a half circles.

The book also tours the observatories themselves: Uraniborg, and the half-buried
Stjerneborg, sunk into the ground so that wind could not shake the instruments.

## Why it matters

Brahe's accuracy is the foundation the seventeenth century built on — Kepler's
laws come out of Brahe's Mars observations, and those observations were only
possible because of the instruments described here. This is the record of how that
precision was achieved: not by a single invention but by decades of iterative
engineering in brass and steel, documented by the man who commissioned it.

It is also, frankly, a self-portrait. Part scientific manual and part proud display, it
was printed to be given to patrons — and it remains the essential record of
pre-telescopic astronomy at its peak.

The translated source is the 1602 printing, opening with an engraved title page,
