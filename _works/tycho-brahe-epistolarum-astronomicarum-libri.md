Before scientific journals existed, astronomers argued, boasted, and traded
discoveries by letter. This volume collects the correspondence with Landgrave
Wilhelm IV of Hesse-Kassel and his gifted observer Christoph Rothmann — a rare
window into science as it actually happened.

## What is in this book

The exchanges defend the Tychonic model of the cosmos against both Ptolemy and
Copernicus, debate the design of instruments, and compare observations between
two rival observatories — Hven and Kassel — as each measured the same sky and
compared results.

Running through them is the argument Brahe pressed hardest against
Copernicanism: that a moving Earth would require the fixed stars to lie at such
distances that, given their apparent sizes, they would have to be impossibly vast.
The same reasoning appears in Riccioli's *Astronomia Reformata* two generations
later, and it was a serious objection — it dissolved only when telescopic optics
were understood well enough to show that the measured stellar discs were
artifacts of the instrument.

What makes the letters valuable is that they are unpolished. Positions are staked
out, conceded, and revised; instruments are praised and criticized; the tone runs
from collegial to combative and back. This is the argument before it hardened into
published doctrine.

## Why it matters

Kassel under Wilhelm IV was the one observatory in Europe that could challenge
Hven on its own terms, and Rothmann was a Copernican. The correspondence is
therefore not a great man explaining himself to admirers but two well-equipped
programs testing each other — the closest thing the sixteenth century had to peer
review.

## About this edition

The translated source is the 1596 printing. Many letters — chiefly the Kassel
correspondence — were printed twice in the original edition: first in their original
German, then in a Latin translation Brahe supplied *propter exteros*, for the
benefit of foreign readers. Each such letter appears here once, translated from the
language in which it was composed, with a bracketed editorial note marking
where a twin has been passed over.
