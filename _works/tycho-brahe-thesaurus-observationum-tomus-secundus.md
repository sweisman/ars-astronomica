For twenty-one years on the island of Hven, and afterwards in Bohemia, Tycho
Brahe and his assistants recorded what they saw. This volume continues the
*Thesaurus Observationum*, the record gathered from the surviving logbooks by
J. L. E. Dreyer for his edition of Tycho's complete works.

## What is in this book

Four years of observation, 1586 to 1589. These were the years when Uraniborg and
its underground companion observatory, Stjerneborg, were fully equipped and in
continuous use. Each year is set out as the logbooks kept it: positions of the
Sun, Moon and planets, and measurements of the fixed stars. An appendix to the
observations of 1589 includes a catalogue of the stars in the zodiac.

## Why it matters

The published books of Tycho Brahe give his conclusions. The logbooks give the
evidence. These are the years of the observatory at its most productive, and the
observational record from which Kepler, working from Tycho's data, derived the
laws of planetary motion.

## About this edition

The source is volume XI of Dreyer's *Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia*
(Copenhagen, 1924), edited with J. Raeder. The volume before it, covering 1563
to 1585 and opening with Dreyer's introduction to the whole series, is
[*Thesaurus Observationum, Tomus Primus*](/works/tycho-brahe-thesaurus-observationum-tomus-primus/).
