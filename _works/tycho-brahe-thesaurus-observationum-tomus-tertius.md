For twenty-one years on the island of Hven, and afterwards in Bohemia, Tycho
Brahe and his assistants recorded what they saw. This volume continues the
*Thesaurus Observationum*, the record gathered from the surviving logbooks by
J. L. E. Dreyer for his edition of Tycho's complete works.

## What is in this book

Six years of observation, 1590 to 1595. Each year is set out as the logbooks kept
it: observations of the Sun and Moon, of Saturn, Jupiter, Mars, Venus and
Mercury, and of the fixed stars in the zodiac and in the northern and southern
constellations.

## Why it matters

The published books of Tycho Brahe give his conclusions. The logbooks give the
evidence: the data from which Kepler derived the elliptical orbit of Mars, and
still the most accurate body of naked-eye positional astronomy ever assembled.
The manuscripts themselves have never been digitized, so Dreyer's edition is one
of only two complete and reliable witnesses to them.

## About this edition

The source is volume XII of Dreyer's *Tychonis Brahe Dani Opera Omnia*
(Copenhagen, 1925). The volumes before it are
[*Thesaurus Observationum, Tomus Primus*](/works/tycho-brahe-thesaurus-observationum-tomus-primus/)
and [*Tomus Secundus*](/works/tycho-brahe-thesaurus-observationum-tomus-secundus/);
the record continues in
[*Tomus Quartus*](/works/tycho-brahe-thesaurus-observationum-tomus-quartus/).
