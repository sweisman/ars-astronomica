The *Triangulorum planorum et sphaericorum praxis arithmetica* is Tycho Brahe's
working manual of trigonometry. Tycho never printed it; it survives in a single
known manuscript at Prague, bound with a copy of Rheticus's table of
trigonometric functions.

## What is in this book

Sixteen numbered rules, seven for plane triangles and nine for spherical, each
showing how to find a triangle's unknown sides and angles by arithmetic, with
worked examples and figures.

## Why it matters

The manual shows the computing that lay behind the reductions of the Uraniborg
observations: the everyday trigonometry by which raw measurements of altitude
and distance became the positions recorded in the
[*Thesaurus Observationum*](/works/tycho-brahe-thesaurus-observationum-tomus-primus/).

## About this edition

The manuscript first appeared in 1886 as a photolithographic facsimile edited by
F. J. Studnička in Prague, and this translation follows that facsimile, in the
Bavarian State Library copy. Studnička took the manuscript for Tycho's autograph;
J. L. E. Dreyer, in his 1913 edition of Tycho's works, argued that the hand is
not Tycho's.
